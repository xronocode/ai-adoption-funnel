# Quality Gates

Each layer transition is defined by a **qualitative** filter, not just a frequency threshold. This turns the funnel from a headcount into a capability model.

| Transition | Quantitative filter | Qualitative gate |
|------------|--------------------|--------------------|
| 1 → 2 | uses AI ≥ few times/year | — |
| 2 → 3 | daily/weekly + Microsoft 16% | **"produces work impossible a year ago"** (80% of Frontier vs 58% of all AI users) |
| 3 → 4 | uses AI for code | **agentic** — multi-file, multi-step, autonomous (not autocomplete) |
| 4 → 5 | seniority ≥ director/C-level | **hands-on** — personally codes, doesn't delegate |
| 5 → 6 | builds own tools | **documented, reusable methodology** (framework, open-source) |
| 6 → 7 | works in regulated industry | **ships under full compliance** — audit trail, model risk, data residency |

## Why qualitative gates

Frequency alone conflates "asked a chatbot daily" with "orchestrates a multi-agent pipeline daily." The gates encode the *kind* of usage, which is what actually separates the layers. If a future survey operationalizes these gates directly (e.g. measures "multi-file autonomous coding" rather than "uses AI"), the funnel becomes a measured model instead of an extrapolated one.
